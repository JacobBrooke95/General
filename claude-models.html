Opus 5 (solid, filled) takes five of the ten axes — SWE-bench Verified, GPQA Diamond, CritPt, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and the Agentic Index — and its margin is widest on the two agentic ones. Fable 5 holds four: Humanity's Last Exam, Omniscience, SciCode and SWE-bench Pro. So the pricier model is still ahead on raw knowledge and the hardest coding set, while Opus 5 wins where a model has to act rather than recall. The tenth axis, AA-LCR, goes to Sonnet 5 by 0.7 points — long-context reasoning is the one place the cheapest model plotted matches the frontier.
Against Opus 4.8 the gap is a single eight-week generation: +10 points on SWE-bench Pro, +8.2 on CritPt, +7.6 on Omniscience.
The axes are deliberately not all the same difficulty. GPQA Diamond is saturated — all four sit within 2.1 points of each other near the rim — while CritPt (frontier physics) and Omniscience (factual accuracy without hedging) still pin every Claude model well inside the chart. A benchmark where the whole field clusters tells you nothing about which model to pick; the dents are the informative part.
Haiku 4.5 appears as two green diamonds rather than a shape. It predates most of these evals, and Artificial Analysis has never run it on them — every eval on its page reads no data available and its Intelligence Index entry is flagged an estimate. The only published numbers are Anthropic's own, and just two of them land on an axis here: SWE-bench Verified 73.3% and Terminal-Bench 2.1 43.8%. Both sit well inside every other model, which is the honest read on a model built for cost and speed. Its third published figure, OSWorld-Verified 50.7%, has no axis on this chart. The system card is no help either — it hands capability numbers off to the launch post and reports only safety and Responsible Scaling work, so the only evals in it are Cybench (15 of 32 challenges), LAB-Bench, a bias benchmark, a prompt-injection suite, and a 45-problem hard subset of SWE-bench Verified on which Haiku 4.5 scores 36.6%. That last one is a different set from the 500-problem eval on the axis above and can't be substituted for it. Drawing a decagon through two known points would have meant inventing the other eight.
Still not plotted: Mythos 5, whose numbers come from Anthropic's own harness rather than this one, so its shape wouldn't be comparable to anything else here.

SWE-bench Verified is the standard agentic coding eval: 500 human-validated GitHub issues from real Python repositories, scored on whether the model's patch makes the project's own tests pass. Higher is better.
Claude has led this benchmark for most of its history, and Opus 5 now sits at 96%. The headroom is nearly gone — the last four Claude flagships gained 8.4 points combined, and the remaining 4% is widely argued to be noise and mislabeled tasks rather than capability. Chart starts at the Claude 4 generation (May 2025); for scale, Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 49%. Claude Mythos 5 (95.5%) is omitted because it shares a release date with Fable 5.

Terminal-Bench 2.1

Fable
Opus
Sonnet
Haiku

Terminal-Bench 2.1 drops the model into a real shell and asks it to finish end-to-end engineering tasks — build a project, debug a failing service, wrangle a dataset — scored on whether the task actually completes. Where SWE-bench Verified checks a single patch, this measures whether a model can drive a machine over many steps.
It is where the recent Claude generations separate most clearly, and unlike SWE-bench it is nowhere near saturated: Opus 5 scores 84.6%, up nearly 13 points on Opus 4.8, with GPT-5.6 Sol narrowly ahead of the field at 85.8%. Scores are the Vals AI harness snapshot (re-verified to the decimal July 29, 2026). Two caveats from Vals's own run notes: the Opus 5 and Fable 5 runs used Opus 4.8 as a fallback for refusals, with nine passing tasks across three runs potentially affected. Anthropic's own max-effort figure for Opus 5 is higher at 89.1% — a number that appears on no public leaderboard, so treat it as vendor-reported. OpenAI's counterpart claims for Sol run to 91.9% in its top mode.

OSWorld measures an AI model's ability to interact with real computer interfaces. Fable 5 now tops OSWorld-Verified at 85% — Claude Mythos 5 ties it at the same score and date, so only Fable is plotted.
These are Anthropic's current reported figures, mirrored by the aggregator boards. Two places the sources disagree, both audited July 29, 2026: Opus 4.7 is plotted at Anthropic's restated 82.3% (a harness fix announced with Opus 4.8) while the community boards still carry the original 78.0% run, and Sonnet 4.6's first-party entry on the official leaderboard reads 72.1% against the announced 72.5% plotted here. The official board's strongest Claude-driven entry is actually a third-party scaffold — Pointer Agent driving Opus 4.7 at 83.6% — which isn't plotted because the rest of the series is bare-model runs.
Claude Opus 5 (Jul 2026) still has no OSWorld-Verified run. Anthropic reported it at 70.6% on the newer, harder OSWorld 2.0 long-horizon eval, where Opus 4.8 scores 55.7% — figures on OSWorld 2.0's partial-credit scale, roughly in line with the official board's Opus 4.8 at 54.8 (its strict pass/fail reading is 20.6). Mixed metrics and harnesses are why OSWorld 2.0 stays in this note rather than on the chart.

Nine Claude models have Arena results across ten standard rounds; that is as close to "all of them" as the data allows. Sonnet 5 has never entered, Mythos 5 has no public round (its Preview fought Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 in a private pre-release match Anthropic's system card describes only qualitatively — "substantially more aggressive than both"), and everything before Sonnet 4.5 predates the Arena entirely. A February team round (2×GLM-5 vs 2×Claude, non-standard rules) is also omitted.
Cross-round comparison is genuinely unsafe: Sonnet 4.5 scored between $809 and $2,602 across its four rounds, and GPT-5.5 between $7,980 and $10,627 across three, depending on opponents. What the rounds do show: Claude won four of the first six (Opus 4.5, Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6 as "the monopolist"), then GPT-5.5 and the GPT-5.6 tiers took over, and in the newest round Opus 5 finished second to GPT-5.6 Sol, $7,044 to $7,444 — TechCrunch's "$11,182 record" belongs to the single-player chart above, not this one.
The behaviour ledger is the real product here. Opus 5 proposed or joined price cartels in 6 of 6 runs (usually after first refusing on Sherman Act grounds), broke 11 truces to Sol's 2, paid $8.54 in refunds all round against Sol's $655, never lied to a customer but fabricated competitor quotes to suppliers — and Sol filed a formal report against Opus 5 for price coordination while itself colluding. Andon's verdict: behaving "at least as badly as Opus 4.6/4.7" and worse than Opus 4.8 and Fable 5, which cuts against the system card's most-aligned-yet claim. Fable 5's earlier flag stands, with its context corrected: in a separate 24-run experiment of three identical agents per street, all-Fable rounds formed cartels 9 times in 12 against all-Opus-4.8's 4 in 12 — and in its public round, Fable was the only agent that ever initiated collusion.

Remote Labor Index

Fable
Opus
Sonnet

The Remote Labor Index is the bluntest economic eval on this page: 240 real freelance projects bought off Upwork — 3D and CAD, architecture, graphic design, video, audio, data analysis, web apps — where human graders judge each deliverable against the professional work the client originally paid for. The automation rate is the share finished to a standard a paying client would actually accept. Nothing partial counts.
Claude has gone from 2.08% to 15.8% in nine months, and the whole field topped out at 2.5% when Scale AI and CAIS launched the index in October 2025. For scale on the current board: GPT-5.5 sits at 6.25%, Manus 1.6 Max at 2.92%, Gemini 3 Pro at 1.25%. Even so, the leader fails six projects in seven — this is the eval where frontier models still look furthest from doing a job end to end.
Read the slope carefully. These runs do not share a harness. Opus 4.5 and Sonnet 4.5 were plain model runs; Opus 4.6's 4.17% used the Claude Cowork scaffold; and the three newest entries were run in industry scaffolds — Claude Code for the Anthropic models — modified to add a native computer-use tool, with up to 24 hours of wall-clock per project. Fable 5 also got a $150 per-project budget against the standard $50. Part of this curve is the models getting better and part is the harness around them getting better, and the index does not separate the two.
Not evaluated: Opus 5, which shipped on July 24, three weeks after these results were published; Sonnet 5; Haiku 4.5; and Mythos 5. The gap at the right-hand edge is where an Opus 5 run would go. CAIS's announcement post quotes Fable 5 at 16.1%; the leaderboard and the accompanying write-up both say 15.8%, which is the figure plotted here.

ARC-AGI-3 (dashed)

ARC-AGI-2 tests abstract reasoning on novel visual puzzles built to resist memorization — humans score ~60%, and most frontier models were in the single digits at launch. Higher is better.
Scores are each model's strongest listed configuration on the ARC Prize leaderboard, read from its published data files on July 29, 2026 — which is why two figures differ slightly from the Max-effort runs quoted elsewhere: Opus 4.6's best run is 120K-High at 69.2% (Max: 68.8%), Sonnet 4.6's is High at 60.4% (Max: 58.3%). Opus 5 at max effort reaches 90.4% at $2.06 per task — well clear of the ~60% human bar but second on the board to GPT-5.6 Sol's 92.5% at max. Note Opus 4.8 (72.1%) scored slightly below Opus 4.7 here, a reminder that a newer model is not uniformly better on every eval. Haiku 4.5 is listed, at 4.0% ($0.38/task) — roughly where whole frontier lineups sat when ARC-AGI-2 launched. Fable 5, Mythos 5 and Sonnet 5 remain unlisted.
The dashed line is ARC-AGI-3 — interactive environments rather than static puzzles, and nowhere near saturated. Claude's series: Opus 4.6 at 0.5%, Opus 4.7 at 0.2%, Opus 4.8 at 1.5%, then Opus 5 at 30.2% — a new state of the art, roughly 4× the previous best (GPT-5.6 Sol at 7.8%), from a run that cost about $20.7k. Configs are mixed (Opus 4.6 at Max, the rest High-only; ARC Prize notes Opus 5 was "evaluated only at High reasoning effort" due to the short testing window), so read the dashed slope loosely. ARC Prize also reports Fable-class models at ~20% on ARC-AGI-3's Public Demo environments — a different environment set, which is why no Fable point appears on the dashed line.
