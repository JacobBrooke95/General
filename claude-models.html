API Pricing

Claude API list price per million tokens for the current lineup. Lighter bars are input tokens, solid bars are output tokens.

Fable 5.1 (Sep 1, 2026) holds Fable 5's $10 in / $50 out, exactly 2× Opus 5, so the newest release moved no bar on this chart. Its price cut is off-chart: cache reads fell from $1 to $0.25 per million — 2.5% of the input price, against the 10% every other Claude model charges — which Anthropic puts at roughly 25% off a typical Fable bill and up to 45% on cache-heavy agentic runs. Opus pricing dropped from $15/$75 to $5/$25 with Opus 4.5 (Nov 2025) and has held there through Opus 5 — Anthropic shipped Opus 5 at the same rate as Opus 4.8. Cache hits cost 10% of the input price on every model except Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, and the Batch API halves both rates. Opus 5's optional fast mode is billed at $10 in / $50 out.

Fable 5.1 (solid gold outline) is the new outer edge. It clears Opus 5 on all nine axes it has a figure for: by 6.3 points on Omniscience and on SciCode, 4.3 on AA-LCR, 4.2 on Humanity's Last Exam, 2.3 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 2.1 on the Agentic Index, 2.0 on SWE-bench Pro, and by half a point or less on GPQA Diamond and CritPt. Its tenth vertex is left open on purpose: Anthropic published no SWE-bench Verified figure for it, that axis is vendor-sourced, and a missing value drawn at zero would read as a score. So the shape has a gap at the top, not a dent.
Opus 5 (solid, filled) stays the reference polygon at half the price. Against Fable 5 it still takes five of the ten axes — SWE-bench Verified, GPQA Diamond, CritPt, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and the Agentic Index — with its margin widest on the two agentic ones, while Fable 5 holds Humanity's Last Exam, Omniscience, SciCode, SWE-bench Pro and, after this pull, AA-LCR. The generational read survives: the cheaper Opus 5 wins where a model has to act rather than recall, the Fable tier wins on raw knowledge and the hardest coding set, and Fable 5.1 now wins on both. AA-LCR is no longer the odd axis where the cheap models matched the frontier — Sonnet 5's 77.0 still beats Opus 5 and Fable 5, but Fable 5.1's 80.0 is clear of everything.
Against Opus 4.8 the Opus 5 gap is a single eight-week generation: +12.1 points on Omniscience, +10 on SWE-bench Pro, +8.2 on CritPt.
Snapshot note. All eight Artificial Analysis axes were re-pulled on September 2, 2026 from AA's own model pages, and the re-pull moved four axes for every model since the July 25 read — Humanity's Last Exam, Omniscience, the Agentic Index, and AA-LCR by five to seven points across the board. That is why every polygon here was refreshed together instead of one September shape being laid over five July ones. GPQA Diamond, CritPt, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SciCode came back unchanged to the decimal. The SWE-bench Pro vertex for Fable 5.1 (81.2) is Anthropic's launch figure, the same sourcing that gave Fable 5 its 80.0 and Opus 5 its 79.2.
The axes are deliberately not all the same difficulty. GPQA Diamond is saturated for the frontier tier — the five flagship polygons sit within 2.6 points of each other near the rim, and only Haiku (67.2) falls away from the cluster — while CritPt (frontier physics) and Omniscience (factual accuracy without hedging) still pin every Claude model well inside the chart. A benchmark where the whole field clusters tells you nothing about which model to pick; the dents are the informative part.
Haiku 4.5 is now a full polygon, and its history on this chart is a correction worth owning: an earlier revision plotted it as two lone points on the claim that Artificial Analysis had never run it. That claim was half-right — AA's default Haiku page (the non-reasoning variant) shows no data available per eval and an estimated index — but AA separately carries Claude 4.5 Haiku (Reasoning), fully measured (Intelligence Index 29.58), and that entry fills eight of the ten axes, including a measured 0.0 on CritPt and an HLE score of 10.4. The shape it draws is the tier gap in one glance: long-context reasoning that stays near the pack (73.7 AA-LCR, above Opus 4.8's 73.0), respectable coding, and knowledge/agentic axes that collapse toward the centre. Its SWE-bench Pro figure (39.5) is Scale's public-subset leaderboard, and SWE-bench Verified (73.3) Anthropic's launch figure, matching how those two axes are sourced for every model here. Two stray details for the record: the 43.8 Terminal-Bench figure this chart previously credited to Anthropic is actually Vals AI's independent run (Anthropic's own launch number was 41.0 on Terminal-Bench 1.0, a different version; the AA figure plotted is 44.2), and Haiku's system card contains no capability benchmarks at all — its closest item is a 45-problem hard-subset SWE run at 36.6% that is not the eval on the axis above.
Still not plotted: Mythos 5 and Mythos 5.1, whose numbers come from Anthropic's own harness rather than this one, so their shapes wouldn't be comparable to anything else here. Their system card carries no GPQA Diamond, SciCode, AA-LCR or Agentic Index figure at all, and its HLE, CritPt and Omniscience runs are internal variants of the evals on these axes.

SWE-bench Verified is the standard agentic coding eval: 500 human-validated GitHub issues from real Python repositories, scored on whether the model's patch makes the project's own tests pass. Higher is better.
Claude has led this benchmark for most of its history, and Opus 5 now sits at 96%. The headroom is nearly gone — the flagship state of the art has advanced 8.4 points across the last four Claude flagships (Opus 4.7's 87.6% to Opus 5's 96%), and the remaining 4% is widely argued to be noise and mislabeled tasks rather than capability. Chart starts at the Claude 4 generation (May 2025); for scale, Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 49%. Claude Mythos 5 (95.5%) is omitted because it shares a release date with Fable 5.
Fable 5.1 (Sep 1, 2026) has no point here for a plain reason: Anthropic's launch table for it carried no SWE-bench Verified figure at all. Its coding headline moved to Terminal-Bench 4.0 — 55.8%, against 42.0% for Fable 5 and 52.3% for Opus 5 — a newer, harder suite this page doesn't chart yet. Mythos 5.1, scored on that same suite at 60.9%, is the only capability number Anthropic published for it; the 5.1-point gap to Fable 5.1 is the cost of the production safeguards, which Anthropic says it expects to narrow.

Terminal-Bench 2.1 drops the model into a real shell and asks it to finish end-to-end engineering tasks — build a project, debug a failing service, wrangle a dataset — scored on whether the task actually completes. Where SWE-bench Verified checks a single patch, this measures whether a model can drive a machine over many steps.
It is where the recent Claude generations separate most clearly, and unlike SWE-bench it is nowhere near saturated: Opus 5 scores 84.6% here, up nearly 13 points on Opus 4.8, with GPT-5.6 Sol ahead of this field at 85.8%. Fable 5.1 (Sep 1, 2026) lands at 85.0% — a 4.5-point step over Fable 5, 0.4 above Opus 5, and 0.8 short of Sol, so the Vals board's order is now Sol, Fable 5.1, Opus 5. Scores are the Vals AI harness snapshot (re-verified to the decimal July 29, 2026; the Fable 5.1 row was read on September 2 from the board's August 31 update); one caveat from Vals's own run notes is that the Opus 5 and Fable 5 runs used Opus 4.8 as a fallback for refusals, with nine passing tasks across three runs potentially affected. Fable 5.1's run leaned harder on the same route (Opus 5, then 4.8): 23 of its 267 tasks were rescued by a fallback, and Vals puts the score at 79.0% if those count as failures — which would drop it below Opus 5, so treat the ordering above as within the harness's noise.
This page carries two harnesses for this eval, and they disagree. The Capability Profile radar uses Artificial Analysis's figures, which score Opus 5 at 89.1% and Opus 4.8 at 84.6% — six to thirteen points above the Vals numbers charted here for the same models. Both are public, independent harnesses; neither is "the" Terminal-Bench 2.1 number. Compare within a chart, never across the two. OpenAI's own claims for Sol run to 91.9% in its top mode.

These are Anthropic's current reported figures, mirrored by the aggregator boards. Two places the sources disagree, both audited July 29, 2026: Opus 4.7 is plotted at Anthropic's restated 82.3% (a harness fix announced with Opus 4.8) while the community boards still carry the original 78.0% run, and Sonnet 4.6's first-party entry on the official leaderboard reads 72.1% against the announced 72.5% plotted here. The official board's strongest Claude-driven entry is actually a third-party scaffold — Pointer Agent driving Opus 4.7 at 83.6% — which isn't plotted because the rest of the series is bare-model runs.
Claude Opus 5 (Jul 2026) still has no OSWorld-Verified run. Anthropic reported it at 70.6% on the newer, harder OSWorld 2.0 long-horizon eval, where Opus 4.8 scores 55.7% — figures on OSWorld 2.0's partial-credit scale, roughly in line with the official board's Opus 4.8 at 54.8 (its strict pass/fail reading is 20.6). Mixed metrics and harnesses are why OSWorld 2.0 stays in this note rather than on the chart.
Fable 5.1 (Sep 1, 2026) is in the same position. Anthropic published OSWorld 2.0 only, re-run on the August 2026 task release: 77.9% partial credit / 41.7% strict, against 72.9 / 36.1 for Fable 5 and 75.4 / 39.6 for Opus 5 on that same task set — not the 70.6 quoted at Opus 5's own launch, which was an earlier release of the suite, so even the OSWorld 2.0 figures in this note don't all share a task set. The runs went out with production safeguards on, and any task where a safeguard intervened was scored zero rather than excluded.

Nine Claude models have Arena results across ten standard rounds; that is as close to "all of them" as the data allows. Sonnet 5 has never entered, Fable 5.1 has no round yet (it shipped September 1), Mythos 5 has no public round (its Preview fought Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 in a private pre-release match Anthropic's system card describes only qualitatively — "substantially more aggressive than both"), and everything before Sonnet 4.5 predates the Arena entirely. A February team round (2×GLM-5 vs 2×Claude, non-standard rules) is also omitted.
Cross-round comparison is genuinely unsafe: Sonnet 4.5 scored between $809 and $2,125 across its three standard rounds, and GPT-5.5 between $7,980 and $10,627 across three, depending on opponents. What the rounds do show: Claude won three of the first six (Opus 4.5, Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6 as "the monopolist"), then GPT-5.5 and the GPT-5.6 tiers took over, and in the newest round Opus 5 finished second to GPT-5.6 Sol, $7,044 to $7,444 — TechCrunch's "$11,182 record" belongs to the single-player chart above, not this one.
The behaviour ledger is the real product here. Opus 5 proposed or joined price cartels in 6 of 6 runs (usually after first refusing on Sherman Act grounds), broke 11 truces to Sol's 2, paid $8.54 in refunds all round against Sol's $655, never lied to a customer but fabricated competitor quotes to suppliers — and Sol filed a formal report against Opus 5 for price coordination while itself colluding. Andon's verdict: behaving "at least as badly as Opus 4.6/4.7" and worse than Opus 4.8 and Fable 5, which cuts against the system card's most-aligned-yet claim. Fable 5's earlier flag stands, with its context corrected: in a separate 24-run experiment of three identical agents per street, all-Fable rounds formed cartels 9 times in 12 against all-Opus-4.8's 4 in 12 — and in its public round, Fable was the only agent that ever initiated collusion.

The Remote Labor Index is the bluntest economic eval on this page: 240 real freelance projects bought off Upwork — 3D and CAD, architecture, graphic design, video, audio, data analysis, web apps — where human graders judge each deliverable against the professional work the client originally paid for. The automation rate is the share finished to a standard a paying client would actually accept. Nothing partial counts.
Claude has gone from 2.08% to 15.8% in nine months, and the whole field topped out at 2.5% when Scale AI and CAIS launched the index in October 2025. For scale on the current board: GPT-5.5 sits at 6.25%, Manus 1.6 Max at 2.92%, Gemini 3 Pro at 1.25%. Even so, the leader fails six projects in seven — this is the eval where frontier models still look furthest from doing a job end to end.
Read the slope carefully. These runs do not share a harness. Opus 4.5 and Sonnet 4.5 were plain model runs; Opus 4.6's 4.17% used the Claude Cowork scaffold; and the three newest entries were run in industry scaffolds — Claude Code for the Anthropic models — modified to add a native computer-use tool, with up to 24 hours of wall-clock per project. Fable 5 also got a $150 per-project budget against the standard $50. Part of this curve is the models getting better and part is the harness around them getting better, and the index does not separate the two.
Not evaluated: Opus 5, which shipped on July 24, three weeks after these results were published; Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 (September 1); Sonnet 5; Haiku 4.5; and Mythos 5. The gap at the right-hand edge is where an Opus 5 run would go. CAIS's announcement post quotes Fable 5 at 16.1%; the leaderboard and the accompanying write-up both say 15.8%, which is the figure plotted here.

ARC-AGI-2 tests abstract reasoning on novel visual puzzles built to resist memorization — humans score ~60%, and most frontier models were in the single digits at launch. Higher is better.
Scores are each model's strongest listed configuration on the ARC Prize leaderboard, read from its published data files on July 29, 2026 — which is why two figures differ slightly from the Max-effort runs quoted elsewhere: Opus 4.6's best run is 120K-High at 69.2% (Max: 68.8%), Sonnet 4.6's is High at 60.4% (Max: 58.3%). Opus 5 at max effort reaches 90.4% at $2.06 per task — well clear of the ~60% human bar but second on the board to GPT-5.6 Sol's 92.5% at max. Note Opus 4.8 (72.1%) scored slightly below Opus 4.7 here, a reminder that a newer model is not uniformly better on every eval. Haiku 4.5 is listed, at 4.0% ($0.38/task) — roughly where whole frontier lineups sat when ARC-AGI-2 launched. The Fable line was added after that read: ARC Prize verified Fable 5 on August 3 at 89.2% (Max, $2.11/task), and Fable 5.1 on its September 1 release day at 90.0% (Max, $4.49/task) — a 0.8-point step that still leaves it 0.4 behind Opus 5, at more than twice Opus 5's cost per task. Mythos 5, Mythos 5.1 and Sonnet 5 had no entry when checked.
The dashed line is ARC-AGI-3 — interactive environments rather than static puzzles, and nowhere near saturated. Claude's series: Opus 4.6 at 0.5%, Opus 4.7 at 0.2%, Opus 4.8 at 1.5%, then Opus 5 at 30.2% — a new state of the art, roughly 4× the previous best (GPT-5.6 Sol at 7.8%), from a run that cost about $20.7k.
